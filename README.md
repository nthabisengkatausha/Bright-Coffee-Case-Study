# Bright-Coffee-Case-Study
Assignment submission

1. Overview of the Case Study
The Bright Coffee Shop Analysis was a comprehensive data audit of three distinct retail locations: Astoria, Hell’s Kitchen, and Lower Manhattan. The goal was to move beyond simple "Total Sales" numbers to understand the operational DNA of each store.

We evaluated the business across four key pillars:

Inventory & Product: Identifying "Hero" products vs. "Underperformers."

Store Benchmarking: Comparing footprint, transaction volume, and average basket value.

Time-Series Trends: Mapping the "Morning Rush" and seasonal monthly shifts.

Pricing Strategy: Segmenting the menu into Budget, Mid-Range, and Premium categories.

2. Methodology: How the Study was Conducted
We utilized a multi-tool approach to ensure data integrity and actionable visualization:

SQL (Data Extraction): We used complex queries to clean ISO-timestamped data and group thousands of transactions. We applied CASE statements to categorize pricing and used DISTINCT counts to separate "Total Items Sold" from "Total Unique Customer Visits."

Excel/Google Sheets (Transformation): We re-ordered alphabetical data into chronological sequences (Jan–Jun) and calculated the Average Basket Value (ABV) to measure upselling success.

Data Visualization: We transformed raw tables into Pie Charts for market share, Stacked Bar Charts for seasonality, and Line Graphs for hourly performance.

3. Key Insights & Findings
Our analysis revealed three major "Business Truths" that were previously hidden:

The Parity Success: Revenue is almost perfectly split (33% each) across all three stores. This means the brand is remarkably stable and not reliant on a single "super-store" to stay afloat.

The "Morning Cliff": The Manhattan stores experience a 50% revenue drop-off at 11:00 AM. Currently, we are over-staffed in the afternoons at these locations, leading to wasted labor costs.

The Astoria Opportunity: Unlike the Manhattan "commuter" stores, Astoria maintains steady traffic all afternoon. It is our strongest candidate for a "Remote Work" loyalty program or afternoon food expansion.

Seasonality: Sales are trending upward toward June (the peak month), but there is a significant "Winter Slump" in January and February that requires aggressive promotional intervention.
